# TS-09 Waves and swell

> SYNTHETIC DATA for retrieval testing.

## Triggers
- Significant wave height 1 m or more.
- Ocean swell coming from a direction ≥ 30° different from the wind.
- Current against the wind (steep, short waves; see TS-06).

## Core idea
Waves change **speed per tack**. One tack is often "into the waves" (slow) and the other "with the waves" (fast). Downwind, waves are a big speed source (surfing). The side choice can depend on **where the water is flatter** (upwind) or **where the waves are best** (downwind).

## Start
- Avoid starting on the tack that goes straight into the waves if both ends are similar.

## Upwind
- Spend more time on the tack where the waves are easier (better speed and height).
- Look for flatter water (behind a breakwater, closer to shore, current with the wind).

## Downwind
- Sail the angle that lets the boat surf the most waves.
- In big swell: steer down the waves, up in the troughs.

## Boat handling
- More power upwind in waves (less flat sails, more twist) to drive through.
- Fitness: wave days are physically harder; plan recovery between races.

## Coach actions
- Report wave height and direction compared with the wind.
- Video downwind technique for debrief.

## Plan B
If waves make one tack very slow, accept a longer route on the fast tack rather than fighting the waves.

## Past lessons (synthetic)
- Port of LA test event, July 2026 (about 1 m swell, 12–18 kt): best downwind legs came from sailing the right side, with the waves at a good angle.
