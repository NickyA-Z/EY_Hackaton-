# TS-03 Strong wind

> SYNTHETIC DATA for retrieval testing.

## Triggers
- Mean wind ≥ 18 kt, or gusts ≥ 24 kt.
- Also apply when the forecast shows a rise of ≥ 6 kt within 2 hours (prepare early).

## Core idea
In strong wind, **boat handling and staying upright decide the race**, not small shifts. Fewer manoeuvres, fewer mistakes. Pressure is everywhere, so the side choice depends on **waves and current**, not on wind strength.

## Start
- Conservative start: mid-line or slightly toward the favoured end, with room to bear away.
- Avoid the crowded end: collisions and capsizes there are costly.
- Be at full speed at the gun; do not stall the boat.

## Upwind
- **Fewer tacks**: only tack for shifts ≥ 10° or to stay clear of traffic.
- Prefer the side with **flatter water** (lee of land, breakwater, or with the current going in the wind direction).
- Approach the windward mark on the starboard layline from a bit further out; laylines are longer because of leeway and waves.

## Downwind
- Safety first in gusts: bear away in puffs, steer for the waves.
- Choose the side with the best wave angle (see TS-09).
- Gybe in the lull or on the top of a wave, never at the bottom of a gust.

## Boat handling
- Depower early (outhaul, cunningham, vang / kicker as per class guide).
- Hiking endurance matters: plan nutrition and water between races.

## Coach actions
- Check equipment for wear before launch (lines, hiking straps, rudder).
- Share the gust pattern (how often, how strong) rather than shift detail.
- **Safety:** above 25 kt mean, discuss with sailors whether to go afloat for training. Follow race committee signals.
- **Inform the organisation** at least 1 hour before an expected jump to ≥ 25 kt gusts.

## Plan B
If gusts exceed 28 kt or the boat is overpowered on every gust: sail the "survival plan": no risky manoeuvres, finish the race, keep the score low.

## Past lessons (synthetic)
- Marseille 2025, day 4 (22–27 kt): two capsizes at the leeward gate cost 30 places in total. New rule: wide gybes, early.
