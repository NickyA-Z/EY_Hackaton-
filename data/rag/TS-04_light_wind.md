# TS-04 Light wind

> SYNTHETIC DATA for retrieval testing.

## Triggers
- Mean wind below 8 kt.
- Also apply when a sea breeze has not yet started (morning racing on sea-breeze days, see TS-07).

## Core idea
In light wind **pressure beats shifts**. 2–3 kt more wind can mean 30–40% more speed. Find the pressure first, then play the shifts inside it. Current has a bigger effect because boats are slow (see TS-06).

## Start
- Start where you can **reach the first pressure** quickly.
- Avoid the second row: dirty air in light wind is very costly.
- Be on the line with speed; do not luff and stall.

## Upwind
- Look upwind for **darker water** (pressure). Sail toward it even on the "wrong" tack.
- Few tacks: each tack costs a lot of speed.
- Watch the fleet ahead: boats heeling or moving faster show the pressure.

## Downwind
- Sail to pressure, then gybe to stay in it.
- Heat up (sail higher) in lulls to keep the boat moving.

## Boat handling
- Smooth, minimal steering; crew weight to leeward/forward for less wetted surface (class-dependent).

## Coach actions
- Report the pressure pattern: where the darker water is and how it moves.
- Watch for the sea-breeze start (wind direction change + temperature drop).

## Plan B
If the wind dies or becomes very patchy (< 4 kt), stay in the middle of the course and avoid both corners: the risk of getting stuck is too big.

## Past lessons (synthetic)
- Medemblik, June 2026, 4–6 kt: the top 3 all went toward a visible pressure line on the left, against the compass logic.
